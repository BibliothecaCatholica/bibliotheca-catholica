The Ecumenical Councils

All twenty-one ecumenical councils of the Catholic Church, from Nicaea to the Second Vatican Council, in order, with what each settled and where to read it.

The first seven councils 325–787

  1. 1

    1. First Council of Nicaea 325

    Taught, against Arius, that the Son is true God, of one substance with the Father; issued the first form of the Nicene Creed; and set a common way to date Easter.

    Percival translation on Wikisource ↗ · Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  2. 2

    2. First Council of Constantinople 381

    Confessed the divinity of the Holy Spirit and completed the Creed in the form still prayed at Mass.

    Percival translation on Wikisource ↗ · Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  3. 3

    3. Council of Ephesus 431

    Rejected the teaching of Nestorius and confessed Mary as Theotokos, Mother of God, because her Son is one divine Person.

    Percival translation on Wikisource ↗ · Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  4. 4

    4. Council of Chalcedon 451

    Defined that Christ is one Person in two natures, divine and human, without confusion or division, receiving the Tome of Pope Leo the Great.

    Percival translation on Wikisource ↗ · Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  5. 5

    5. Second Council of Constantinople 553

    Condemned the writings known as the Three Chapters and confirmed the teaching of Chalcedon against a Nestorian reading of it.

    Percival translation on Wikisource ↗ · Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  6. 6

    6. Third Council of Constantinople 680–681

    Taught that Christ has two wills, divine and human, the human will always following the divine, against monothelitism.

    Percival translation on Wikisource ↗ · Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  7. 7

    7. Second Council of Nicaea 787

    Upheld the veneration of holy images, distinguishing the honor shown to them from the worship due to God alone.

    Percival translation on Wikisource ↗ · Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

Councils of the Middle Ages 869–1517

  1. 8

    8. Fourth Council of Constantinople 869–870

    Deposed Photius and restored Ignatius as patriarch of Constantinople, and confirmed the veneration of images. It is counted as ecumenical in the West.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  2. 9

    9. First Lateran Council 1123

    Confirmed the Concordat of Worms, which settled the dispute over lay investiture of bishops, and passed canons of reform.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  3. 10

    10. Second Lateran Council 1139

    Ended the schism of the antipope Anacletus II and passed canons on the discipline and celibacy of the clergy.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  4. 11

    11. Third Lateran Council 1179

    Required a two-thirds majority of the cardinals to elect a pope and passed canons against simony and heresy.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  5. 12

    12. Fourth Lateran Council 1215

    Professed the faith in a creed that uses the word transubstantiation, required yearly confession and Easter Communion, and called a crusade.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  6. 13

    13. First Council of Lyon 1245

    Declared the Emperor Frederick II deposed and called for help for the Holy Land.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  7. 14

    14. Second Council of Lyon 1274

    Received a short-lived reunion with the Greek Church, taught that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son, and set rules for the conclave.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  8. 15

    15. Council of Vienne 1311–1312

    Suppressed the Knights Templar and taught that the rational soul is of itself the form of the human body.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  9. 16

    16. Council of Constance 1414–1418

    Ended the Western Schism with the election of Martin V and condemned the teachings of Wycliffe and Hus.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  10. 17

    17. Council of Basel, Ferrara, and Florence 1431–1445

    Agreed decrees of reunion with the Greeks, Armenians, Copts, and others, and defined the primacy of the Roman Pontiff.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  11. 18

    18. Fifth Lateran Council 1512–1517

    Taught that each human soul is individual and immortal, and passed reforms on the eve of the Reformation.

    Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

Councils of the modern era 1545–1965

  1. 19

    19. Council of Trent 1545–1563

    Answered the Protestant Reformation on Scripture and Tradition, justification, and the seven sacraments, and began the reform of the clergy and seminaries.

    Canons and decrees at Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  2. 20

    20. First Vatican Council 1869–1870

    Taught that God can be known by the light of natural reason, and defined the primacy of the pope and his infallibility when he teaches ex cathedra.

    Dei Filius and Pastor Aeternus at Papal Encyclicals Online ↗

  3. 21

    21. Second Vatican Council 1962–1965

    Renewed the liturgy and set out the Church’s teaching on revelation, on the Church herself, and on her place in the modern world, in sixteen documents.

    All sixteen documents at vatican.va ↗

    In the reading room: Sacrosanctum Concilium, Lumen Gentium, Dei Verbum, Gaudium et Spes
