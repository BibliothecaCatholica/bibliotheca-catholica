Subject

  • Confessions Read here

    Augustine of Hippo · Spiritual life

    Augustine looks back on his conversion and examines memory, desire, time, and the interpretation of Genesis in thirteen books addressed to God.

  • On Christian Doctrine Read here

    Augustine of Hippo · Church Fathers

    Augustine's guide to interpreting Scripture and teaching its meaning: things and signs, use and enjoyment, figurative language, and Christian eloquence, in a preface and four books (De Doctrina Christiana).

  • Rule of Saint Benedict

    Benedict of Nursia · Monastic life

    A rule for monastic community, organizing prayer, work, authority, hospitality, and daily conduct. The linked Doyle translation is distinct from earlier English editions.

  • The Imitation of Christ Read here

    Thomas à Kempis · Spiritual life

    Short meditations on humility, the interior life, following Christ, and the Eucharist, traditionally arranged in four books.

  • Story of a Soul Read here

    Thérèse of Lisieux · Spiritual life

    Thérèse’s autobiographical manuscripts describe her childhood, Carmelite vocation, and way of confidence and love. This early English edition differs from later critical translations.

  • The Life of Saint Teresa of Jesus Read here

    Teresa of Ávila · Spiritual life

    Teresa recounts her life, experiences of prayer, and the beginnings of her reform. This is her autobiography, a different work from Interior Castle.

  • Spiritual Exercises: adapted retreat edition Read here

    Ignatius of Loyola · Spiritual life

    Charles Coppens adapts Ignatian exercises for an eight-day Jesuit retreat and shorter renewal retreats. This is a retreat manual rather than an unqualified edition of Ignatius’s original text.

  • Revelations of Divine Love Read here

    Julian of Norwich · Spiritual life

    Julian reflects on visions received during illness, exploring divine love, sin, suffering, and hope. Translation and editorial choices vary between editions.

  • Abandonment; or, Absolute Surrender to Divine Providence Read here

    Jean-Pierre de Caussade · Spiritual life

    Spiritual counsels associated with Jean-Pierre de Caussade on trusting divine providence and responding faithfully to the duties of the present moment.

  • The Practice of the Presence of God

    Brother Lawrence · Spiritual life

    Conversations and letters associated with Brother Lawrence on attending to God in ordinary work and daily life.

  • Pensées

    Blaise Pascal · Apologetics

    Pascal’s unfinished notes for an apology for Christianity consider human greatness and misery, reason, faith, and diversion. Arrangement and numbering vary by edition.

  • Apologia pro vita sua Read here

    John Henry Newman · Apologetics

    Newman explains the development of his religious convictions and his passage from Anglicanism to the Catholic Church.

  • An Essay in Aid of a Grammar of Assent Read here

    John Henry Newman · Philosophy

    Newman investigates how people reason, give assent, and reach certainty, with particular attention to religious belief.

  • An Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine Read here

    John Henry Newman · Theology

    Newman considers how Christian doctrine develops over time and proposes ways to distinguish authentic development from corruption.

  • The Idea of a University Read here

    John Henry Newman · Education

    Discourses and essays on liberal education, the unity of knowledge, and the place of theology in a university.

  • Orthodoxy Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1908 · Apologetics

    Chesterton presents an imaginative defense of Christian belief through paradox, wonder, moral limits, and his own intellectual journey.

  • The Everlasting Man Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · Apologetics

    An interpretation of human history and the coming of Christianity, written as an alternative to accounts that reduce religion to an evolutionary stage.

  • The Innocence of Father Brown Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · Literature

    The first collection of Father Brown detective stories, beginning with The Blue Cross. This volume does not contain the complete Father Brown series.

  • The Man Who Was Thursday Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · Literature

    A comic and unsettling novel about a poet who enters an anarchist conspiracy. Its subtitle, A Nightmare, signals the story’s changing imaginative register.

  • Saint Francis of Assisi Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · Biography

    Chesterton’s portrait of Francis of Assisi considers poverty, joy, creation, and the saint’s historical setting. This is separate from his book on Thomas Aquinas.

  • Europe and the Faith Read here

    Hilaire Belloc · History

    Belloc argues for the central role of the Catholic Church in the formation of Europe. Read it as a historical interpretation, distinct from his travel narrative The Path to Rome.

  • Divine Comedy Read here

    Dante Alighieri · Literature

    Dante’s poetic journey through Hell, Purgatory, and Heaven. The selected Longfellow translation is one English rendering; explanatory notes are useful for a first reading.

  • The Betrothed Read here

    Alessandro Manzoni · Literature

    Manzoni’s historical novel follows two young people whose planned marriage is disrupted amid political oppression, war, and plague in seventeenth-century Lombardy.

  • Lord of the World Read here

    Robert Hugh Benson · Literature

    Benson’s speculative novel imagines a future conflict between a universal secular movement and the Catholic Church.

  • Come Rack! Come Rope! Read here

    Robert Hugh Benson · Literature

    A historical novel of English Catholics facing persecution during the reign of Elizabeth I, exploring conscience, vocation, and fidelity.

  • Poems of Gerard Manley Hopkins Read here

    Gerard Manley Hopkins · Poetry

    Poems from the 1918 collection edited by Robert Bridges, including Hopkins’s distinctive rhythms, nature imagery, and religious verse.

  • Heretics Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1905 · Apologetics

    Twenty essays answering Kipling, Shaw, Wells, and other modern writers, arguing that a person's ultimate beliefs are the most practical thing about him. Its challenge led directly to Orthodoxy.

  • What's Wrong with the World Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1910 · Philosophy

    Chesterton's case for the family, the home, and small property against both big business and state socialism, with chapters on imperialism, feminism, and education.

  • The Ball and the Cross Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1909 · Literature

    A Catholic Highlander and an atheist editor agree to settle their quarrel with swords, and spend the novel being prevented from fighting by a modern world that thinks religion does not matter.

  • The Wisdom of Father Brown Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1914 · Literature

    The second collection of Father Brown stories, twelve mysteries in which the priest-detective's knowledge of sin and human nature finds the truth.

  • The Incredulity of Father Brown Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1926 · Literature

    The third Father Brown collection, eight stories in which apparent miracles, curses, and oracles are explained.

  • The Secret of Father Brown Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1927 · Literature

    The fourth Father Brown collection, eight mysteries set inside a frame story in which the priest explains his method. This volume does not contain the complete Father Brown series.

  • The Ballad of the White Horse Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1911 · Poetry

    A long narrative poem on King Alfred's Christian stand against the Danes, with the Virgin Mary's counsel at its center; a favorite of Tolkien's generation.

  • The Catholic Church and Conversion Read here

    G. K. Chesterton · 1926 · Apologetics

    Chesterton's short account, written four years after his own reception, of the stages by which a person approaches, resists, and finally enters the Catholic Church.

  • The Servile State Read here

    Hilaire Belloc · 1912 · History

    Belloc's argument that capitalism, unable to last, was sliding toward a society where most people would be secured but unfree, and his case for the widely distributed property of the Catholic social tradition.

  • The Catholic Church and History Read here

    Hilaire Belloc · 1926 · History

    A brief rebuttal of the historical arguments raised against the Church's claim to teach with divine authority, from the papacy and persecutions to the Reformation.

  • Loss and Gain: The Story of a Convert Read here

    John Henry Newman · 1848 · Literature

    Newman's first novel follows Charles Reding through Oxford in the years of the Tractarian controversies until he becomes a Catholic, a lightly told mirror of Newman's own path.

  • Callista: A Tale of the Third Century Read here

    John Henry Newman · 1855 · Literature

    A historical novel set in North Africa during the Decian persecution, in which a Greek sculptor of pagan idols comes to faith in Christ.

  • The Dream of Gerontius Read here

    John Henry Newman · 1865 · Poetry

    Newman's dramatic poem on the death of a faithful man, his judgment, and his entry into Purgatory, later set to music by Edward Elgar.

  • Summa Theologiae Read here

    Thomas Aquinas · 1265–1274 (this translation 1911–1925) · Theology

    The complete English Summa: God and creation, the moral life in general and in particular, and Christ and the sacraments, with the Supplement that completes the Third Part, read question by question and article by article.

  • On Prayer and the Contemplative Life Read here

    Thomas Aquinas · Spiritual life

    Selections from the Summa Theologica on prayer, the contemplative and active lives, and the religious state, translated and arranged as a single book.

  • The City of God Read here

    Augustine of Hippo · Theology

    Augustine's answer to pagans who blamed Christianity for the sack of Rome, growing into a history and theology of two cities, one built by love of self and one by love of God.

  • Ecclesiastical History of the English People Read here

    Bede the Venerable · History

    Bede's history of the conversion of the English, from Roman Britain and Augustine of Canterbury's mission to the saints and councils of his own day. Bede is a Doctor of the Church.

  • Saint Catherine of Siena as Seen in Her Letters Read here

    Catherine of Siena · Spiritual life

    A selection of Catherine's letters to her family, disciples, rulers, and popes, arranged with introductions that trace her life and her work for the Church. Catherine is a Doctor of the Church.

  • The Consolation of Philosophy Read here

    Boethius · Philosophy

    Written in prison while awaiting execution, Boethius's dialogue with Lady Philosophy on fortune, happiness, providence, and free will shaped Christian thought for a thousand years.

  • The Writings of the Apostolic Fathers Read here

    Clement of Rome, Ignatius of Antioch, Polycarp, and others · Church Fathers

    The earliest Christian writings after the New Testament: Clement's letter to the Corinthians, the letters of Ignatius and Polycarp, the Martyrdom of Polycarp, the Epistle of Barnabas, the Shepherd of Hermas, and related texts.

  • The Glories of Mary Read here

    Alphonsus Liguori · 1750 · Spiritual life

    Alphonsus's commentary on the Salve Regina and meditations on the feasts, sorrows, and virtues of the Blessed Virgin, one of the most widely read Marian books. Alphonsus is a Doctor of the Church.

  • Paradoxes of Catholicism Read here

    Robert Hugh Benson · 1913 · Apologetics

    Lenten sermons by the convert priest and novelist on the apparent contradictions of the Church: peace and war, wealth and poverty, faith and reason, authority and liberty.

  • Mere Christianity

    C. S. Lewis · 1952 · Apologetics

    An introduction to Christian belief and practice, moving from moral reasoning to God, Christian conduct, and the Trinity. The linked excerpt discusses the moral law.

  • The Screwtape Letters

    C. S. Lewis · 1942 · Literature

    Satirical letters from a senior demon to an apprentice explore temptation, prayer, pride, and the small habits of daily life.

  • The Abolition of Man

    C. S. Lewis · 1943 · Philosophy

    Three lectures on education, objective value, and the consequences of treating human nature as material to be controlled.

  • The Great Divorce

    C. S. Lewis · 1945 · Literature

    An imaginative bus journey from a grey town toward Heaven explores freedom, attachment, and the refusal of joy. It is a literary vision rather than a systematic account of the afterlife.

  • The Problem of Pain

    C. S. Lewis · 1940 · Apologetics

    A philosophical and theological treatment of suffering, considering divine goodness, human freedom, and pain. It differs in approach from Lewis’s later personal reflections in A Grief Observed.

  • Miracles

    C. S. Lewis · 1947 · Apologetics

    An argument about the possibility of miracles and the assumptions behind naturalism, leading to a discussion of the Incarnation and Christian belief.

  • The Four Loves

    C. S. Lewis · 1960 · Spiritual life

  • Till We Have Faces

    C. S. Lewis · 1956 · Literature

    A retelling of the Cupid and Psyche myth through the voice of Psyche’s sister Orual, exploring possessive love, self-knowledge, and the encounter with the divine.
