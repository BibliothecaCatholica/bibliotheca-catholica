11. Code

The HTML templates, JavaScript, CSS, Python tools, and tests in this repository are released under the MIT License. Copyright (c) 2026 Bibliotheca Catholica contributors.

The MIT License covers code only. It does not cover the data files the code reads, the writing it displays, the hosted texts, or any third-party material described below, even where those files sit in the same folders.

22. Open data (CC0)

The datasets in data/open/ are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0 (data/open/LICENSE.txt). CC0 covers our compilation, normalization, and curation: our one-line summaries, notes, rights labels, matches, and Wikidata links.

The reading catalog metadata in data/library.json (work and edition records, contributor roles, rights labels, checked dates, notes, and checksums) is also dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0, and data/open/LICENSE.txt names it in its scope. It was checked on 2026-10-26 and contains no Wikipedia text. CC0 does not extend to the texts those records point to.

CC0 does not cover any third-party material described in section 5. In particular, the Wikipedia excerpts in data/catholic-hub.json and data/authors-detail.js are not CC0, and they are not included in data/open/.

33. Site writing (CC BY 4.0)

Text written for this site, such as the About page, section introductions, edition guides, reading-list notes, editorial context notes, and the rights and policy pages, is licensed under CC BY 4.0. Please credit "Bibliotheca Catholica" with a link to https://bibliothecacatholica.com.

44. Hosted texts

The texts in data/texts/ and at https://texts.bibliothecacatholica.com are editions checked as public domain in the United States. We claim no rights in them and add no license; there is no blanket license over them. Each Project Gutenberg file keeps its full Project Gutenberg License and credits, and those terms apply to the file as distributed. "Project Gutenberg" is a registered trademark of the Project Gutenberg Literary Archive Foundation. Outside the United States, check your local law.

55. Third-party material

  • Wikipedia. Author excerpts are shortened from the English Wikipedia article named and linked beside them (shown only on the pages of authors with a hosted work), and are available under CC BY-SA 4.0. They are stored in the extract field of data/catholic-hub.json and data/data.js. They are carved out of the MIT License and of CC0, and are not part of the open data.
  • Wikimedia Commons and Wikipedia images. Author portraits are shown only with a credit taken from the image's file page (author where named, license, and a link to the file page), recorded in data/portrait-credits.json. That file is the credit record: for each author portrait it gives the file page, the license, artist (the credit name as the site shows it, "Author unknown" when the file page names no one), artistRaw (the author text exactly as the file page gives it), and shown (whether the portrait appears); its fields key explains each field. Each image stays under the license on its file page. Images marked non-free (fair use) are not shown.
  • Project Gutenberg. Texts and catalog snapshots from Project Gutenberg follow the Project Gutenberg License and terms of use.
  • Isidore Index. The Index reproduces catalog information from the St. Isidore e-book library (isidore.co). Files listed there are held by isidore.co, not by this site; Bibliotheca Catholica does not host, and does not vouch for, any file at isidore.co.
  • Natural Earth. The Rites & Liturgy map is drawn from Natural Earth country and state boundaries, which are in the public domain (terms of use). Made with Natural Earth. Borders are illustrative and imply no position.
  • Facts and links. Names, titles, dates, ISBNs, and links are facts.
