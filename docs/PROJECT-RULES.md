# Detailed project rules

Mandatory for relevant changes. Migrated without removing the existing rules on November 9, 2026. Paths in code spans are repository-root-relative. Read [AGENTS](../AGENTS.md) and [CENTRAL](../CENTRAL.md) first. Dated implementation descriptions are historical context; current code and explicit owner decisions govern current state.

## Content rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Hosting rule (owner rule change, November 15, 2026; see CENTRAL.md).** There is no fixed publication-date cutoff. Host a full text only when that edition's basis is recorded with evidence; the wording and the moving year live once in `tools/rights_rule.py` (`pd_last_year()`, `RULE_SENTENCE`), so never write a cutoff year into copy, asserts, or tests.
   - **Bases** (`rightsBasis` on the edition, from `BASES`): `pd-date`, public domain in the US by date (the default: in 2026, first published 1930 or earlier; the line moves forward each January 1); `pd-nonrenewal`, a work first published in the United States from 1931 to 1963 whose copyright was never renewed. Its source record must hold `nonRenewal` with the first publication's year, place, publisher, and country ("US"); a dated "no renewal found" from the Stanford database and from NYPL's renewal data, and from the Copyright Office's own records for a book of 1950 or later; the HathiTrust rights code, quoted exactly, if there is one; a sentence saying how an earlier edition abroad was ruled out (a foreign first edition could have had its US copyright restored in 1996); and the scan the text follows, which must be that very printing. `nonrenewal_problems()` in `tools/rights_rule.py` checks it, and each batch waits for the owner's review (rule 14); `pd-defect`, published without notice or with a defective notice; `open-license`, with the license named and its text kept; `permission`, with the grant quoted and dated in `data/sources/`. Any basis but `pd-date` needs `rightsEvidence` on the edition (`tools/build_library.py` checks it).
   - **Sources.** A text may come from any source (Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks, Wikisource, Internet Archive scans, isidore.co, Faded Page, and others). The rights belong to the edition, not the website: a site in another country may hold texts still protected in the US, so check each edition's first US-relevant publication, not the host's label. Where a source asserts a status, quote it; never upgrade or paraphrase it. Where a source's own terms conflict with our habits, the source wins.
   - **Editions are separate rights objects.** Name the translation and the printing, not only the work; a 1910 translation and a 1975 revision get separate records. Keep the copyright page, publisher line, translator credit, and front-matter notices with the text.
   - **Fail closed.** An edition with no recorded basis, or one in copyright without documented permission, is link only: never hosted in whole or in part. Takedown requests are honored first and the record corrected afterward.
2. **Gutenberg notice (owner rule change, November 14, 2026; see CENTRAL.md).** The notice is the Project Gutenberg License's own sentence from clause 1.E.1 ("This eBook is for the use of anyone anywhere in the United States ... before using this eBook."), **verbatim; never paraphrase or shorten it**. "Project Gutenberg™ License" is linked to the full licence (`PG_LINE` in `tools/notices.py`, the same words in `scripts/library.js`; `tests/test_text_pages.py` checks them against `data/texts/pg-130.txt`). It appears once per page, at the end of the text in small type: after a chapter or Summa page's text, at the end of the reader's last section, and at the end of the EPUB's last chapter. It is not repeated in the edition details, above the text, or per section. The hosted TXT, downloads, the source front matter in the reader, print, and EPUBs also keep the full license. Other sources (Standard Ebooks, CCEL, and the Supplement transcription) keep their own notices in the same place.
   - **Quality labels** (the formatting pilot note, automatic layout, OCR comparison or "OCR uncertain" counts, and "not a manual proofread") appear only in the edition details: the reader's "Source, rights, and downloads" panel and the "Edition details" toggle after the text on chapter and Summa pages. On chapter and Summa pages the first sentence of the text sits within the first screen.
3. **Reading room first.** "Read here" is the primary way to read. Isidore EPUB and PDF downloads are an alternate format ("Other formats"), not the headline, and appear only where the rights badge allows (rule 10).
4. **Editorial flags.** Fringe, conspiracy, and sedevacantist titles in the Isidore Index stay listed (removing them feeds the conspiracy), but each gets a calm, factual context note, the Dignitatis Humanae 1 and 3 disclaimer quoted verbatim from vatican.va, and a link to the Fidelity & Editorial Policy (`#/policy`, `/policy/`). Listing is not endorsement. Sedevacantist works are labeled as sedevacantist. Do not add Nostra Aetate quotes without the owner's approval.
5. **Flagged titles are never featured.** They must not appear on the home page, in reading lists, edition guides, reading options, reading-room matches, recommendations, the sitemap, or static pages. Flagged book pages carry `noindex`.
6. **Search defaults to "Everything"** (selected works rank first; the scopes are Everything, Selected library only, and Isidore Index only).

13. **Automatic display-only layout (2026-11-14).** Automatic, display-only prose reflow and underscore italics are allowed on hosted editions under these safeguards. `tools/auto_layout.py` (part of `npm run build`) decides, per hosted edition, which TXT blocks may reflow on screen and which paired `_underscores_` render as emphasis, and writes `data/auto-layout/<edition>.json` bound to the TXT's SHA-256. Every other edition is on, and its coverage (the share of multi-line blocks reflowed) is listed in `data/auto-layout/report.json`; the 50% cut-off was dropped on November 14. ## Files that must never be deleted or moved

**Document archive:** `BibliothecaCatholica/bibliotheca-texts` serves a delivery copy of the hosted TXT files (117 hosted editions in `data/library.json` as of 2026-11-15; `python3 tools/hosted_counts.py` gives the current figure, and the repository's `manifest.json` lists what has been exported) at `https://texts.bibliothecacatholica.com/texts/`. The main reader prefers that host and falls back to local `data/texts/`. Keep local originals and provenance. `textArchive` in `data/library.json` controls this behavior. Export offline with `python3 tools/build_text_archive.py --output /absolute/path/to/bibliotheca-texts`; review the manifest and checksums before publishing the separate repository. Do not bulk mirror unreviewed imported books. See `docs/archive/PROJECT-REVIEW-2026-10-22.md`.

**Link-only editions (v14 pattern):** a public-domain scan or text that is not on the validated Gutenberg path (Internet Archive, Fordham, and similar) is an edition with `access: "full-text"` and a `source` URL but no `text`, like the New Advent and Vatican editions. Its card says "Full text at source" and it is never downloaded or mirrored. Leave out `added` on link-only works so **Recently added** ("New in the reading room") lists only texts readable here.

**Adding a hosted Gutenberg text (v10 pattern):**
1. Check the specific edition or translation year in the Gutenberg front matter (it needs a recorded basis under rule 1: in 2026, first published 1930 or earlier, or written evidence of no renewal, a defective notice, an open license, or permission; record the evidence in the edition `note`).
2. `python3 tools/fetch_gutenberg_edition.py <PG number> ...` downloads the original `pgN.txt` bytes to `data/texts/pg-N.txt`, snapshots the catalog record to `data/sources/pg-N.json`, and prints the SHA-256. It never edits `data/library.json` and skips releases with no plain-text file.
3. Add the work and edition to `data/library.json` by hand (`label`, `rights`, `checked`, `note`, `sourceTitle`, `text`, `sha256`), with `"added": "YYYY-MM-DD"`. The `added` date drives the **Recently added** section on Discover & Read and a "New" card badge for 90 days.
4. `python3 tools/fetch_librivox.py` rebuilds `data/librivox.json/.js` and the per-recording chapter lists in `data/librivox/<id>.json` that power the reader's persistent **Listen** dock. Use the work's `audioTitles` array for LibriVox title aliases; leave out a recording that is clearly a different work or an abridgment by listing its LibriVox id in the work's `audioExclude` array (for example, Butler's one-volume *Lives of the Saints with Reflections*, #5324, is excluded from the full Kenedy volumes).
5. `npm run build`, update test counts, then re-export and push `bibliotheca-texts` (step above) so the text is served from the texts subdomain.

`CNAME`, `.nojekyll`, `.well-known/atproto-did` (the Bluesky handle; leave it unchanged), `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `404.html`, `assets/og-image.png`, `data/texts/`, `data/sources/`, and `_headers` (Cloudflare Pages host-scoped `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` for `*.pages.dev` only; GitHub Pages ignores it). **Never add a bare `/*` noindex rule to `_headers`** — that would noindex production if the file were ever served by a host that applies unscoped rules.
