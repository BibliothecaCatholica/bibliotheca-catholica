Book of the Week: On Christian Doctrine

Augustine's handbook for reading Scripture well, and the book behind "the gold of Egypt"

St. Augustine wrote On Christian Doctrine (De doctrina christiana) for anyone who has to read the Bible and then explain it to others. It is a teacher's handbook, and a surprisingly practical one. Its four books ask two questions: how do we find the meaning of Scripture, and how do we share it once we have found it?

Why read it. Book I sets the goal for everything that follows: all Scripture aims at the love of God and of neighbour. Books II and III deal with words and signs: languages, translations, figures of speech, and the famous counsel that whatever is true in pagan learning belongs to God and may be put to Christian use. Book IV is Augustine the former professor of rhetoric, explaining how a Christian should teach, delight, and move a listener.

Where to begin. Start with Book II, chapters 18 to 42. It is about an hour of reading and connects directly to this week's essay. Then go back to Book I for the foundation. Save Book IV for when you are preparing to teach or write.

Which edition. For a free, reliable English text, use James Shaw's translation in the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers (1887), linked below. It is older English but clear. If you buy a modern translation, check that it includes all four books.

One question to carry. If every reading of Scripture must build up love, how should that change the way we read a passage we find difficult?

Whoever, then, thinks that he understands the Holy Scriptures, or any part of them, but puts such an interpretation upon them as does not tend to build up this twofold love of God and our neighbor, does not yet understand them as he ought.

— St. Augustine, On Christian Doctrine, Book I, ch. 36 (§40); trans. James Shaw, Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, First Series, vol. 2 (1887)

Liturgical note. Advent is a season of listening for the Word. Augustine's little handbook is about exactly that: learning to hear Scripture rightly, so that we can receive the Word made flesh with understanding and with love.
