By Jerome. 1 edition, with a text you can read on this site.

About this work

This is Jerome's Latin prologue to his Commentary on Isaiah, written for the virgin Eustochium around 408 to 410. He says he turns to Isaiah after finishing his work on the twelve prophets and on Daniel, and that he is keeping a promise made to Eustochium's mother, Paula, and to her brother Pammachius. The text is the prologue only, from J. P. Migne, Patrologia Latina, volume 24 (Paris: Petit-Montrouge, 1845), printed columns 17-22. The wording follows the Corpus Corporum edition at the University of Zurich and was collated word for word against the 1845 Migne scan.

No English translation published before 1931 is known, so none is hosted here. The prologue includes the line ignoratio Scripturarum, ignoratio Christi est ("ignorance of the Scriptures is ignorance of Christ"), which has its own stable page at /work/jerome-isaiah-prologue/ignoratio-scripturarum/. Jerome presents Isaiah not only as a prophet but as evangelist and apostle, and he writes as one who would imitate the householder who brings forth things new and old.

From the text

Si enim juxta apostolum Paulum (II Cor. I, 24) Christus Dei virtus est, Deique sapientia; et qui nescit Scripturas, nescit Dei virtutem ejusque sapientiam: ignoratio Scripturarum, ignoratio Christi est.

From the opening of the prologue, in the hosted 1845 Migne Latin text.
