Revelations of Divine Love

Julian of Norwich. Julian reflects on visions received during illness, exploring divine love, sin, suffering, and hope. Translation and editorial choices vary between editions.

By Julian of Norwich. 1 edition, with a text you can read on this site.

about 61,000 words · about 4 hours 20 minutes of reading

About this edition

Project Gutenberg #52958: Grace Warrack’s version (London: Methuen, 1901). Julian’s eighty-six chapters, divided as the hosted text heads them; Warrack’s introduction stays in the reader. No words were changed.

Chapters

  1. I. A Revelation of Love, in Sixteen Shewings
  2. II. A simple creature unlettered. Which creature afore desired three gifts of God
  3. III. I desired to suffer with Him
  4. IV. I saw ... as it were in the time of His Passion.... And in the same Shewing suddenly the Trinity filled my heart with utmost joy
  5. V. God, of Thy Goodness, give me Thyself; only in Thee I have all
  6. VI. The Goodness of God is the highest prayer, and it cometh down to the lowest part of our need
  7. VII. The Shewing is not other than of faith, nor less nor more
  8. VIII. In all this I was greatly stirred in charity to my fellow-Christians that they might see and know the same that I saw
  9. IX. If I look singularly to myself, I am right nought
  10. X. God willeth to be seen and to be sought: to be abided and to be trusted
  11. XI. All thing that is done, it is well done: for our Lord God doeth all." "Sin is no deed
  12. XII. The dearworthy blood of our Lord Jesus Christ as verily as it is most precious, so verily it it most plenteous
  13. XIII. The Enemy is overcome by the blessed Passion and Death of our Lord Jesus Christ
  14. XIV. The age of every man shall be acknowledged before him in Heaven, and every man shall be rewarded for his willing service and for his time
  15. XV. It is not God's will that we follow the feeling of pains in sorrow and mourning for them
  16. XVI. A Part of His Passion
  17. XVII. How might any pain be more to me than to see Him that is all my life, and all my bliss, and all my joy suffer?
  18. XVIII. When He was in pain, we were in pain
  19. XIX. Thus was I learned to choose Jesus for my Heaven, whom I saw only in pain at that time
  20. XX. For every man's sin that shall be saved He suffered, and every man's sorrow and desolation He saw, and sorrowed for Kinship and Love
  21. XXI. We be now with Him in His Pains and His Passion, dying. We shall be with Him in Heaven. Through learning in this little pain that we suffer here, we shall have an high endless knowledge of God which we could never have without that
  22. XXII. The Love that made Him to suffer passeth so far all His Pains as Heaven is above Earth
  23. XXIII. The Glad Giver" "All the Trinity wrought in the Passion of Jesus Christ
  24. XXIV. Our Lord looked unto His [wounded] Side, and beheld, rejoicing.... _Lo! how I loved thee_
  25. XXV. I wot well that thou wouldst see my blessed Mother...." "Wilt thou see in her how thou art loved?
  26. XXVI. It is I, it is I
  27. XXVII. Often I wondered why by the great foreseeing wisdom of God the beginning of sin was not hindered: for then, methought, all should have been well." "Sin is behovable, [playeth a needful part], ; but all shall be well
  28. XXVIII. Each brotherly compassion that man hath on his fellow Christians, with charity, it is Christ in him
  29. XXIX. How could all be well, for the great harm that is come by sin to the creature?
  30. XXX. Two parts of Truth: the part that is open: our Saviour and our salvation; and the part that is hid and shut up from us: all beside our salvation
  31. XXXI. The Spiritual Thirst (which was in Him from without beginning) is desire in Him as long as we be in need, drawing us up to His Bliss
  32. XXXII. There be deeds evil done in our sight, and so great harms taken, that it seemeth to us that it were impossible that ever it should come to good end." "That Great Deed ordained ... by which our Lord God shall make all things well
  33. XXXIII. It is God's will that we have great regard to all His deeds that He hath done, but evermore it needeth us to leave the beholding what the Deed shall be
  34. XXXIV. All that is speedful for us to learn and to know, full courteously will our Lord shew us
  35. XXXV. I desired to learn assuredly as to a certain creature that I loved.... It is more worship to God to behold Him in _all_ than in any special thing
  36. XXXVI. My sin shall not hinder His Goodness working.... A deed shall be done, as we come to Heaven, and it may be known here in part; though it be truly taken for the general Man, yet it excludeth not the special. For what our good Lord will do by His poor creatures, it is now unknown to me
  37. XXXVII. In every soul that shall be saved is a Godly Will that never assented to sin, nor ever shall.", "For failing of Love on our part, therefore is all our travail
  38. XXXVIII. In Heaven “the token of sin is turned to worship.” Examples thereof
  39. XXXIX. Sin is the sharpest scourge.... By contrition we are made clean, by compassion we are made ready, and by true longing towards God we are made worthy
  40. XL. True love teacheth us that we should hate sin only for love." "To me was shewed no harder hell than sin." "God willeth that we endlessly hate the sin and endlessly love the soul, as God loveth it
  41. XLI. _I am the Ground of thy beseeching._" "Also to prayer belongeth thanking
  42. XLII. Prayer is a right understanding of that fulness of joy that is to come, with accordant longing and sure trust
  43. XLIII. Prayer uniteth the soul to God
  44. XLIV. God is endless, sovereign Truth, Wisdom, Love, not-made; and man's Soul is a creature in God which hath the same properties made
  45. XLV. All heavenly things and all earthly things that belong to Heaven are comprehended in these two judgments
  46. XLVI. It is needful to see and to know that we are sinners: wherefore we deserve pain and wrath." "He is God: Good, Life, Truth, Love, Peace: His Clarity and His Unity suffereth Him not to be wroth
  47. XLVII. We fail oftentimes of the sight of Him, and anon we fall into our self, and then find we no feeling of right, nought but contrariness that is in our self
  48. XLVIII. I beheld the property of Mercy, and I beheld the property of Grace: which have two manners of working in one love
  49. XLIX. Where our Lord appeareth, peace is taken, and wrath hath no place." "Immediately is the soul made at one with God when it is truly set at peace in itself
  50. L. The blame of our sin continually hangeth upon us." "In the sight of God the soul that shall be saved was never dead, nor ever shall be dead
  51. LI. He is the Head, and we be His members." "Therefore our Father nor may nor will more blame assign to us than to His own Son, precious and worthy Christ
  52. LII. We have now matter of mourning: for our sin is cause of Christ's pains; and we have, lastingly, matter of joy: for endless love made Him to suffer
  53. LIII. In every soul that shall be saved is a Godly Will that never assented to sin, nor ever shall." "Ere that He made us He loved us, and when we were made we loved Him
  54. LIV. Faith is nought else but a right understanding, with true belief and sure trust, of our Being: that we are in God, and God is in us: Whom we see not
  55. LV. Christ is our Way", "Mankind shall be restored from double death
  56. LVI. God is nearer to us than our own soul" "We can never come to full knowing of God till we know first clearly our own Soul
  57. LVII. In Christ our two natures are united
  58. LVIII. All our life is in three: 'Nature, Mercy, Grace.' The high Might of the Trinity is our Father, and the deep Wisdom of the Trinity is our Mother, and the great Love of the Trinity is our Lord
  59. LIX. Jesus Christ that doeth Good against evil is our Very Mother: we have our Being of Him where the Ground of Motherhood beginneth, with all the sweet Keeping by Love, that endlessly followeth.
  60. LX. The Kind, loving, Mother
  61. LXI. By the assay of this falling we shall have an high marvellous knowing of Love in God, without end. For strong and marvellous is that love which may not, nor will not, be broken for trespass
  62. LXII. God is Very Father and Very Mother of Nature: and all natures that He hath made to flow out of Him to work His will shall be restored and brought again into Him by the salvation of Mankind through the working of Grace
  63. LXIII. As verily as sin is unclean, so verily is it unkind", a disease or monstrous thing against nature. "He shall heal us full fair.
  64. LXIV. _Thou shalt come up above._" "A very fair creature, a little Child, nimble and lively, whiter than lily
  65. LXV. The Charity of God maketh in us such a unity that, when it is truly seen, no man can part himself from other
  66. LXVI. All was closed, and I saw no more." "For the folly of feeling a little bodily pain I unwisely lost for the time the comfort of all this blessed Shewing of our Lord God
  67. LXVII. The place that Jesus taketh in our soul He shall never remove from, without end: for in us His homliest home and His endless dwelling." "Our soul can never have rest in things that are beneath itself, yet may it not abide in the beholding of its self
  68. LXVIII. He said not: _Thou shalt not be tempested, thou shalt not be travailed, thou shalt not be afflicted_; but He said: _Thou shalt not be overcome_
  69. LXIX. I was delivered from the Enemy by the virtue of Christ's Passion
  70. LXX. Above the Faith is no goodness kept in this life, as to my sight, and beneath the Faith is no help of soul; but _in_ the Faith, _there_ willeth the Lord that we keep us
  71. LXXI. Three manners of looking seen in our Lord's Countenance
  72. LXXII. As long as we be meddling with any part of sin we shall never see clearly the Blissful Countenance of our Lord
  73. LXXIII. Two manners of sickness that we have: impatience, or sloth; despair, or mistrustful dread
  74. LXXIV. There is no dread that fully pleaseth God in us but reverent dread
  75. LXXV. We shall see verily the cause of all things that He hath done; and evermore we shall see the cause of all things that He hath permitted
  76. LXXVI. The soul that beholdeth the fair nature of our Lord Jesus, it hateth no hell but sin
  77. LXXVII. Accuse not thyself overmuch, deeming that thy tribulation and thy woe is all thy fault." "All thy living is penance profitable." "In the remedy He willeth that we rejoice
  78. LXXVIII. Though we be highly lifted up into contemplation by the special gift of our Lord, yet it is needful to us to have knowledge and sight of our sin and our feebleness
  79. LXXIX. I was taught that I should see mine own sin, and not other men’s sin, except it may be for comfort and help of my fellow-Christians
  80. LXXX. Himself is nearest and meekest, highest and lowest, and doeth all." "Love suffereth never to be without Pity
  81. LXXXI. God seeth all our living a penance: for nature-longing of our love is to Him a lasting penance in us." "His love maketh Him to long
  82. LXXXII. In falling and in rising we are ever preciously kept in one Love
  83. LXXXIII. Life, Love, and Light
  84. LXXXIV. Charity
  85. LXXXV. Lord, blessed mayest Thou be, for it is thus: it is well
  86. LXXXVI. Love was our Lord's Meaning
